## 1. Feature Governance & Behavior Specification

The **Student Self-Service Assistance** feature provides optional, manual-backed guidance to students before they submit a formal concern in SCMS.

### Core Operational Rules
1. **Suggest / Guidance Only:** When a student describes a question or concern, SCMS displays relevant procedures, requirements, responsible offices, and exact Student Manual page references.
2. **Strictly Optional:** Students are never forced to follow self-service steps before submitting a concern.
3. **No Automatic Ticket Creation:** Viewing self-service guidance does **not** automatically create a concern record in the database.
4. **No Automatic Resolution:** Viewing or acknowledging guidance does **not** automatically resolve or close any existing concern.
5. **Feedback & Seamless Escalation:** Students can mark whether the guidance was helpful and immediately proceed to the existing formal SCMS concern submission and routing workflow if they still need assistance.
6. **Sensitive & Urgent Concern Safeguard:** Topics involving sexual harassment, physical violence, threats, hazing, serious disciplinary complaints, or medical emergencies are **never** treated as ordinary "do-it-yourself" self-help. Instead, SCMS displays the official reporting channel and responsible authorities specified in the Student Manual and provides direct access to formal concern submission and emergency/in-person reporting.

### A1. Replacement of Lost School ID

**Common Student Question:**
"I lost my school ID. How do I get a replacement ID and which offices do I need to visit?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**
The school ID is non-transferable, must be worn at all times inside the campus, and is issued by the University Library. To replace a lost school ID, a student must secure a notarized Affidavit of Loss and process a School ID Issuance Form through the Student Affairs Office (SAO), their College Dean/Chairperson, the Civil Security Unit (CSU) Head, the Cashier, and the University Library.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Secure an Affidavit of Loss from any licensed lawyer available.
2. Go to the Student Affairs Office (SAO) and present the Affidavit of Loss.
3. Receive the School ID Issuance Form from the SAO.
4. Go to your College Dean or Department Chairperson for their signature on the form.
5. Go to the Civil Security Unit (CSU) Head for the signing of the form.
6. Go to the Cashier for payment of the ID replacement fee.
7. Go to the University Library for the issuance of the replacement school ID.

**Requirements:**
- Notarized Affidavit of Loss from a licensed lawyer
- School ID Issuance Form (issued by SAO)
- Signatures of the College Dean/Chairperson and CSU Head
- Official receipt of payment from the Cashier

### A2. Non-Wearing of School ID and Prohibited ID Acts

**Common Student Question:**
"What happens if I forget to bring or wear my school ID on campus, or if I borrow a classmate's ID?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**
Every student must wear their valid school ID at all times inside the campus ("No school ID, no entry" also applies to the library). Non-wearing of the school ID is subject to progressive disciplinary sanctions from the 1st to the 4th offense. Furthermore, using another student's school ID or study load, lending one's school ID, changing the picture on the ID, or tampering with the ID to make it appear as one's own are strictly prohibited and classified as serious offenses involving dishonesty.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. If cited for a **1st offense** of non-wearing of school ID: An authorized University official issues a verbal warning and logs/reports the infraction to the College Head/Dean or SAO.
2. If cited for a **2nd offense**: The student must submit a written explanation bearing their parent's or guardian's signature, after which the Head/Dean or SAO issues a written reprimand.
3. If cited for a **3rd offense**: The Head/Dean, SAO, or authorized official sends the repeated offender home for the day, which is recorded as an official absence.
4. If cited for a **4th offense**: The Head/Dean, SAO, or authorized official recommends the student for investigation for approval of a one-week suspension from class (recorded as official absences).

**Requirements:**
- Validated school ID worn visibly at all times inside the campus
- For 2nd offense of non-wearing: Written explanation signed by the student's parent or guardian

**Responsible Office:**
Civil Security Unit (CSU), Student Affairs Office (SAO), and College Dean/Chairperson

**Source:** Student Manual, pages 64â€“65, 68 (PDF pages 73â€“74, 78)

**Validation Needed:**

4. Upon approval by the SAO, pay the required fee for the Exemption Card at the Cashier.
5. Submit the official receipt to the SAO as proof of payment.
6. Claim your Uniform Exemption Card from the SAO and present it to the security guard upon entering the University.

**Requirements:**

  - Pregnant women
  - Married students
  - Students legitimately working with entities, firms, or establishments not owned by the applicant's family or relatives
  - Students over 35 years of age
  - Cross-enrollees
  - Second coursers
  - Special cases

  - Accomplished Uniform Exemption Application Form (available at SAO)
  - Two (2) 2x2 colored pictures
  - Authenticated marriage certificate (for married women)
  - Authenticated photocopy of Permit to Cross-Enroll (for cross-enrollees)
  - NSO (now PSA) Birth Certificate (for age verification)

### A4. School Uniform Schedule, Dress Code, and Temporary Non-Uniform Entry

**Common Student Question:**
"What days are we required to wear the school uniform, when is wash day, and what if I am a new student who doesn't have a uniform yet?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**
Students from any college or department with a duly approved prescribed uniform must wear their complete uniform from Mondays through Fridays, except on Wednesdays (and on days specifically designated by their respective colleges). Old students must wear their complete uniform one (1) week after the opening of classes, while new students must wear their complete uniform two (2) weeks after classes begin. Students not in proper uniform are refused entry unless endorsed by their Dean and approved by the SAO.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Wear the complete prescribed college uniform on Mondays, Tuesdays, Thursdays, and Fridays (Wednesday is the designated non-uniform/civilian attire day, unless otherwise designated by your college; PE uniform is worn on PE class days).
2. Observe the University dress code at all times: skirt length must not be more than one (1) inch above the knee, and prohibited attire (slippers, sandals, *bakya*, sleeveless shirts/*sando*, miniskirts, thin-strap blouses, halter backs, or revealing clothing) is strictly disallowed.
3. If you have a legitimate temporary reason for not wearing the prescribed uniform outside the initial grace period, secure a written endorsement from your College Dean and present it to the Student Affairs Office (SAO) for approval to enter the campus.

**Requirements:**
- For temporary non-uniform entry exception: Endorsement from the College Dean and approval from the Student Affairs Office (SAO)

**Responsible Office:**
Student Affairs Office (SAO), College Dean's Office, and Civil Security Unit (CSU)

### B1. Borrowing Library Books and Materials

**Common Student Question:**
"How do I borrow books from the library, how many books can I borrow, and how long can I keep them?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**

5. When leaving the library, open and show your bag, folders, and large envelopes for security inspection, and present the due date card of every library material being taken outside.

**Requirements:**

### C6. Withdrawal from Subjects or Program and Dropping from Class

**Common Student Question:**
"How do I officially withdraw from a subject so I don't get a failing or Dropped (DR) mark, and how much tuition will I be charged?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**
A student may officially withdraw from a subject, even after the close of the enrollment period, upon the recommendation of the College Dean and with the approval of the University Registrar. Withdrawal from subjects is **not ordinarily approved after the midterm examination**. Failure to officially withdraw when stopping class attendance results in a mark of **Dropped (DR)**. Conversely, a teacher-initiated **Dropping from Class** requires the recommendation of the teacher and approval of the Dean.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. Consult your College Dean, Department Chairperson, or the Registrar regarding the withdrawal before the midterm examination.
2. Ensure you meet the two mandatory conditions for withdrawal approval:
   - Your accumulated absences in the class have **not** reached 20% of the officially scheduled class meetings; and
   - You have **written permission to withdraw** from your parents or guardian.
3. Secure the recommendation of your College Dean and submit the withdrawal request for approval by the University Registrar (if withdrawing a dissolved subject, process it before the prelim examination).
4. Settle the applicable withdrawal charges at the Accounting Department based on when the withdrawal is filed:
   - **During enrollment period:** Registration fees only.
   - **Within the 1st week of classes:** 10% of total tuition fees plus registration fees.
   - **Within the 2nd week of classes:** 20% of total tuition fees plus registration fees.
   - **After the 2nd week of classes:** Full (100%) charges regardless of whether or not the student attended classes.

**Requirements:**
- Written permission to withdraw from parents or guardian
- Accumulated absences must be less than 20% of scheduled classes
- Filed prior to the midterm examination
- Recommendation of the College Dean and approval of the University Registrar

**Responsible Office:**
College Dean / Department Chairperson, Registrar's Office, and Accounting Department

**Source:** Student Manual, pages 19â€“20, 26, 31â€“32 (PDF pages 28â€“29, 35, 40â€“41)

### D4. Class Attendance, Tardiness, Cut System, and Teacher Absence Waiting Rule

   - **10 minutes** for a 1-hour class;
   - **15 minutes** for a 1.5-hour class; or
   - **30 minutes** for a 3-hour class.
2. If the teacher does not show up within the elapsed waiting time, the class **Beadle** must inform the **College Dean** so a substitute teacher can be designated immediately.
3. If no substitute teacher is designated within the next **five (5) minutes**, the students may consider themselves officially dismissed.

### G1. Securing a Student Activity Permit and Reserving Campus Venues / Audio-Visual Equipment

**Common Student Question:**
"Our student organization or class wants to hold an event on campus and use a function room or sound system. How do we get an Activity Permit?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**
Only duly accredited student organizations (or authorized class/college representatives) are allowed to conduct activities. An Activity Permit must be secured from the SAO at least **one (1) week before** the actual date of an on-campus activity. In the **Lapu-Lapu and Mandaue (UCLM)**, Banilad, and Mambaling campuses, the **Building Administration Office** manages the reservation of function rooms, audio-visual rooms, sound systems, and university buses. No student activity is allowed one (1) school week before Prelim, Midterm, Semi-final, or Final examinations.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
1. **At least one (1) week before the activity:** The authorized representative of the student organization, classroom, or college secures the **Activity Permit Form** and **Waiver/Quitclaim** from the Student Affairs Office (SAO).

### G4. Off-Campus Activities (Educational Tours / Field Trips) and Rights of Non-Participating Students

**Common Student Question:**
"Am I required to join an off-campus field trip or educational tour, and what happens to my grade if I cannot join?"

**Manual-Based Answer:**
Consistent with CHED Memorandum Order No. 63, series of 2017, **participation in any off-campus activity is strictly voluntary and not compulsory**. An off-campus activity cannot be made a substitute for a major examination to compel students to join. Furthermore, the proponent/instructor **must provide a complementary or substitute activity** for students who cannot participate or opt not to join. A non-participating student **must not be given a failing mark** for not joining the off-campus trip and shall instead be graded based on their accomplishment of the substitute activity.

**Step-by-Step Procedure:**
- **If you are opting NOT to join an off-campus activity:**
  1. Inform your subject instructor/proponent that you cannot participate in the off-campus activity.
  2. Request and accomplish the required complementary/substitute academic activity so you can be graded fairly without penalty.
- **If you ARE participating in an approved off-campus activity:**
  1. Attend the mandatory pre-activity consultation/orientation and pre-departure briefing (covering destination, itemized costs with no hidden charges, and emergency preparedness).
  2. Secure and submit a **Medical Clearance** issued by the University Medical Clinic.
  3. Submit a **duly notarized Parent's/Guardian's Permit/Consent** (failure to submit this notarized consent disallows participation).
  4. Ensure individual or group insurance is arranged and complete the post-activity debriefing and learning/observation journal after returning.

**Requirements:**
- For participating students: University Clinic Medical Clearance, duly notarized Parent's/Guardian's Permit/Consent, individual/group insurance, and observation/learning journal
- For proponents/organizers: SAO Activity Permit submitted at least 1 month prior, notarized Certificate/Report of Compliance submitted at least 15 days prior, 1:35â€“50 teacher-student ratio, accredited tour guides, and valid vehicle registration/insurance

**Responsible Office:**
Subject Proponent / College Dean's Office, Student Affairs Office (SAO), Medical Clinic, and Campus Director / Vice Chancellor for Academics/Administration
