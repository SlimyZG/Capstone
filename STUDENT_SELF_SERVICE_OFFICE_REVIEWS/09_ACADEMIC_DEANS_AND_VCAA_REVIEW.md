### Entry D4 â€” Class Attendance, Tardiness, Cut System, and Teacher Absence Waiting Rule *(Primary Reviewer: Academic Deans & VCAA; Secondary: Registrar)*

#### B.3 Extracted Entry Details
- **Entry ID:** `D4`
- **Topic:** Class Attendance, Tardiness, Cut System, and Teacher Absence Waiting Rule
- **Common student question:** "How many absences or lates are allowed before getting a failing/dropped mark, and how long should our class wait if the teacher hasn't arrived?"
- **Current manual-based answer:**
  - **Tardiness:** Arriving within 15 minutes after the second bell is marked **late**. Arriving more than 15 minutes after the second bell is marked **absent**. Three (3) instances of tardiness equal one (1) cut (absence).
  - **Maximum Absences / Cut System:** Absences must not exceed 20% of prescribed class periods (10 absences for a 3-unit lecture or 1-unit lab course; or $3 \times \text{weekly meetings} + 1$). Exceeding the allowable cuts results in a mark of **Dropped (DR)** or an automatic failure grade of **5.0** *(see **Issue 1** above)*.
  - **When a Teacher is Absent / Late:** Students must wait quietly inside the classroom for the required waiting time before the class Beadle notifies the Dean.
- **Existing step-by-step procedure (When the Teacher Does Not Arrive on Time):**
  1. Students must wait quietly inside the classroom for:
     - **10 minutes** for a 1-hour class;
     - **15 minutes** for a 1.5-hour class; or
     - **30 minutes** for a 3-hour class.
  2. If the teacher does not show up within the elapsed waiting time, the class **Beadle** must inform the **College Dean** so a substitute teacher can be designated immediately.
  3. If no substitute teacher is designated within the next **five (5) minutes**, the students may consider themselves officially dismissed.
- **Existing requirements:**
  - Compliance with the mandatory classroom waiting time and notification of the College Dean by the class Beadle before leaving
- **Responsible office according to the manual:** College Dean's Office / Department Chairperson

### Entry G4 â€” Off-Campus Activities (Educational Tours / Field Trips) and Rights of Non-Participating Students *(Co-Primary Reviewer: Academic Deans/VCAA & SAO; Secondary: Medical Clinic)*

#### B.4 Extracted Entry Details
- **Entry ID:** `G4`
- **Topic:** Off-Campus Activities (Educational Tours / Field Trips) and Rights of Non-Participating Students
- **Common student question:** "Am I required to join an off-campus field trip or educational tour, and what happens to my grade if I cannot join?"
- **Current manual-based answer:** Consistent with CHED Memorandum Order No. 63, series of 2017, **participation in any off-campus activity is strictly voluntary and not compulsory**. An off-campus activity cannot be made a substitute for a major examination to compel students to join. Furthermore, the proponent/instructor **must provide a complementary or substitute activity** for students who cannot participate or opt not to join. A non-participating student **must not be given a failing mark** for not joining the off-campus trip and shall instead be graded based on their accomplishment of the substitute activity.
- **Existing step-by-step procedure:**
  - **If you are opting NOT to join an off-campus activity:**
    1. Inform your subject instructor/proponent that you cannot participate in the off-campus activity.
    2. Request and accomplish the required complementary/substitute academic activity so you can be graded fairly without penalty.
  - **If you ARE participating in an approved off-campus activity:**
    1. Attend the mandatory pre-activity consultation/orientation and pre-departure briefing (covering destination, itemized costs with no hidden charges, and emergency preparedness).
    2. Secure and submit a **Medical Clearance** issued by the University Medical Clinic.
    3. Submit a **duly notarized Parent's/Guardian's Permit/Consent** (failure to submit this notarized consent disallows participation).
    4. Ensure individual or group insurance is arranged and complete the post-activity debriefing and learning/observation journal after returning.
- **Existing requirements:**
  - For participating students: University Clinic Medical Clearance, duly notarized Parent's/Guardian's Permit/Consent, individual/group insurance, and observation/learning journal
  - For proponents/organizers: SAO Activity Permit submitted at least 1 month prior, notarized Certificate/Report of Compliance submitted at least 15 days prior, 1:35â€“50 teacher-student ratio, accredited tour guides, and valid vehicle registration/insurance
- **Responsible office according to the manual:** Subject Proponent / College Dean's Office, Student Affairs Office (SAO), Medical Clinic, and Campus Director / Vice Chancellor for Academics/Administration

#### B.6 Extracted Entry Details
- **Entry ID:** `C6`
- **Topic:** Withdrawal from Subjects or Program and Dropping from Class
- **Common student question:** "How do I officially withdraw from a subject so I don't get a failing or Dropped (DR) mark, and how much tuition will I be charged?"
- **Current manual-based answer:** A student may officially withdraw from a subject, even after the close of the enrollment period, upon the recommendation of the College Dean and with the approval of the University Registrar. Withdrawal from subjects is **not ordinarily approved after the midterm examination**. Failure to officially withdraw when stopping class attendance results in a mark of **Dropped (DR)**.      - Your accumulated absences in the class have **not** reached 20% of the officially scheduled class meetings; and
     - You have **written permission to withdraw** from your parents or guardian.
  3. Secure the recommendation of your College Dean and submit the withdrawal request for approval by the University Registrar (if withdrawing a dissolved subject, process it before the prelim examination).
  4. Settle the applicable withdrawal charges at the Accounting Department based on when the withdrawal is filed (Registration fee only during enrollment; 10% in 1st week; 20% in 2nd week; 100% after 2nd week).
- **Existing requirements:**
  - Written permission to withdraw from parents or guardian
  - Accumulated absences must be less than 20% of scheduled classes

  - Recommendation of the College Dean and approval of the University Registrar

- **Existing requirements:**
  - Must be a candidate for graduation during the current school year (for regular cross-enrollment)
  - Subject must not be offered in the college or must conflict with other required subjects
