### Entry C6 â€” Withdrawal from Subjects or Program and Dropping from Class *(Co-Primary Reviewer: Registrar & Academic Deans; Secondary: Accounting)*

#### B.6 Extracted Entry Details
- **Entry ID:** `C6`
- **Topic:** Withdrawal from Subjects or Program and Dropping from Class
- **Common student question:** "How do I officially withdraw from a subject so I don't get a failing or Dropped (DR) mark, and how much tuition will I be charged?"
- **Current manual-based answer:** A student may officially withdraw from a subject, even after the close of the enrollment period, upon the recommendation of the College Dean and with the approval of the University Registrar. Withdrawal from subjects is **not ordinarily approved after the midterm examination**. Failure to officially withdraw when stopping class attendance results in a mark of **Dropped (DR)**. Conversely, a teacher-initiated **Dropping from Class** requires the recommendation of the teacher and approval of the Dean.
- **Existing step-by-step procedure:**
  1. Consult your College Dean, Department Chairperson, or the Registrar regarding the withdrawal before the midterm examination.
  2. Ensure you meet the two mandatory conditions for withdrawal approval:
     - Your accumulated absences in the class have **not** reached 20% of the officially scheduled class meetings; and
     - You have **written permission to withdraw** from your parents or guardian.
  3. Secure the recommendation of your College Dean and submit the withdrawal request for approval by the University Registrar (if withdrawing a dissolved subject, process it before the prelim examination).
  4. Settle the applicable withdrawal charges at the Accounting Department based on when the withdrawal is filed:
     - **During enrollment period:** Registration fees only.
     - **Within the 1st week of classes:** 10% of total tuition fees plus registration fees.
     - **Within the 2nd week of classes:** 20% of total tuition fees plus registration fees.
     - **After the 2nd week of classes:** Full (100%) charges regardless of whether or not the student attended classes.
- **Existing requirements:**
  - Written permission to withdraw from parents or guardian
  - Accumulated absences must be less than 20% of scheduled classes
  - Filed prior to the midterm examination
  - Recommendation of the College Dean and approval of the University Registrar
- **Responsible office according to the manual:** College Dean / Department Chairperson, Registrar's Office, and Accounting Department
- **Manual page reference:** Student Manual, pages 19â€“20, 26, 31â€“32 (PDF pages 28â€“29, 35, 40â€“41)

### Entry C9 â€” Cross-Enrollment and Summer Study in Another School *(Co-Primary Reviewer: Registrar & Academic Deans)*

#### B.9 Extracted Entry Details
- **Entry ID:** `C9`
- **Topic:** Cross-Enrollment and Summer Study in Another School
- **Common student question:** "Can I cross-enroll or take a summer subject in another school if the subject is not offered this semester?"
- **Current manual-based answer:** No student may enroll simultaneously in two schools without the prior approval of the University Registrar.
  - **Cross-Enrollment:** A Permit to Cross-Enroll is issued by the Registrar only upon the recommendation of the College Dean, and **only if** the applicant is a candidate for graduation during the school year and the subject is not offered in the college during that school year or is in conflict with other subjects. During the summer term, the maximum cross-enrollment load is six (6) units.
  - **Summer Study in Another School:** Allowed only upon prior recommendation of the Dean/Chairperson at a duly authorized school. However, **no permit** will be granted for (a) any subject the student previously failed at UC, or (b) any major, specialized, or professional subject in any year level.
- **Existing step-by-step procedure:**
  1. Verify with your Department Chairperson / College Dean that you meet the eligibility rules (graduating student with an unoffered/conflicting subject for cross-enrollment; or non-failed, non-major subject for summer study).
  2. Secure a written recommendation from your College Dean (and/or Department Chairperson).
